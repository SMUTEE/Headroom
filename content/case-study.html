The premise

Screens are cheap. Judgment isn't.

Anyone can produce a polished interface now. What a founder actually needs to know before handing over a product problem is narrower: can you take something ambiguous, decide what not to build, and ship a version that survives the edge cases.

So the lab is one coherent company rather than a set of demos. The same accounts appear across every build with the same history, which means each surface has to agree with the others. That constraint is most of the value. It is also where the bugs come from.

Build one · Pricing and packaging

If we change this price, what happens to the customers we already have?

A pricing page answers what something costs. It cannot answer the question a founder actually loses sleep over, which is what a change does to the book of business they already have. That answer is a distribution, not a number.

A pricing change is not a revenue number. It is a distribution, and the tail is the part that emails you.

Build two · Activation

Are new customers reaching value, or just finishing our checklist?

Every team agrees that a signup is not an activation, and most dashboards still measure the checklist. The gap between the two is where trials die quietly, because the product has already stopped asking those accounts for anything.

Activation here means one specific thing: create an account health rule and view the accounts it returns. Configuration plus consumption. Following instructions is not value received, and a definition vague enough to be satisfied by anything measures nothing.

The cohort moving

  1. Open Activation. Hold on the four metrics for a beat.
  2. Select Slate Labs. Click Record on each of the two remaining steps.
  3. Cut back to the metrics as they recompute. No narration.

That last point is a design decision with teeth. While building this I removed the activatedAt column from the account model. Storing activation while also deriving it invites the two to disagree the first time anything is backfilled or replayed, and then the number nobody can explain is the one the team plans against.

Underneath

The colour system is computed, and it fails the build.

Both surfaces share one design system. Its palette is generated rather than picked: six ramps in OKLCH with constant hue, vividness peaking mid-ramp, and chroma set as a proportion of each hue's own in-gamut maximum so amber does not come out washed out beside red.

The steps are defined by role, not lightness, so step nine is the solid fill in both light and dark and no component CSS branches on theme. Only the primitives swap.

The part that earns its place is the gate. Every rendered colour pair is measured on every run, and the build fails when one misses its threshold. Pairs declare a content type rather than a ratio, because getting those thresholds right is the expertise: holding a decorative separator to 3:1 forces a heavy grey line nobody wanted, and exempting a focus ring from it ships an accessibility failure.

Three I kept

The bugs are the case study.

Finished work argues for itself badly. What a change in these is worth reading, because each one shows the difference between software that looks right and software that is.

The cent that rounded the wrong way

Converting dollars to cents with Math.round(dollars * 100) returns 100 for 1.005, where the right answer is 101. 1.005 * 100 is 100.49999999999999 in IEEE 754, and no careful rounding afterwards recovers it, because the multiply introduced the error. The fix is to never multiply: shift the decimal point on the number's own string and do integer arithmetic. My own test caught it, which is the only reason it is a note here rather than a support ticket.

A meter that said “consumed” and showed a forecast

The customer's usage bar filled from the projection while labelled as consumption. An account that had used 23% of its allowance rendered a full red bar. It passed every test, because the tests checked the arithmetic and the arithmetic was right. The bar now fills from actual usage with the allowance crossing marked on the same track.

A projection nobody could reproduce

The page showed usage over seven days and a projection built on the exact 6.5 days elapsed. A reader checking the sum by hand landed 8% away from the published figure with no way to account for the gap. It took three passes to fully close, because each fix left one input still computed from a more precise number than the one on screen. On a surface whose job is to make a bill checkable, close enough is the bug.

Honestly

What this does not prove.

Every build in the lab carries a section like this. It is not a disclaimer. Naming what a piece of work does not establish is what makes the rest of it credible.

  • A synthetic environment. It demonstrates billing logic and billing UX, not production financial reconciliation. No tax, no multi-currency, no dunning.
  • The activation event is designed, not discovered. A real one is found by correlating candidate events against retention, and nothing here does that.
  • Nothing about working inside an existing codebase, with its constraints and its history.
  • Nothing about what collaborating on a problem like this is actually like. That needs a different kind of evidence than a lab can produce.
